Research · 2021 to present

From fewer leads to explainable beats

Each project answered the question the previous one left open. The thread running through all of them: cardiac monitoring that is cheap enough for a wearable and transparent enough for a clinician to check.

12021–2023 · MASc

Fewer leads, same diagnosis

The MASc work asked how much of a 12-lead ECG a wearable actually needs. It showed that a carefully selected six-lead subset keeps most of the diagnostic information while cutting sampling time by over 90% at negligible accuracy loss. Selective lead integration then tuned that accuracy-to-cost trade-off, and CardioDiverse, a balanced dataset, linked heart diseases to the leads most relevant to them.

22026 · IEEE SOCC

A five-Gaussian template for the edge

Each beat is modeled with five Gaussians, one per P, Q, R, S and T deflection. A median template built from normal beats is subtracted from every new beat, and the Sparsity Ratio (residual energy over beat energy) flags departures. A drift gate refits the template only after three consecutive drift crossings, so the model follows slow physiological change without learning the anomalies it is meant to catch. No labels are needed at deployment.

Component-local scoring: detect the abnormal beat and name the wave

The fix is in the denominator. Each component is scored against its own template window and normalized by that window's energy, so a P-wave distortion counts in full even when the QRS is untouched. A beat is flagged if any of six tests (the full beat plus five components) crosses its normal-only threshold, and the component with the largest standardized deviation localizes the abnormality. There is no trained classifier and no abnormal label anywhere in the pipeline. The statistic is shown to equal the matched-subspace residual plus a gain-mismatch penalty, and the union rule comes with a distribution-free per-test false-alarm bound.
